# Singularities in a glass of champagne

> [!significance] Speaker
> [Vatsal Sanjay](https://vatsalsanjay.com) ([CoMPhy Lab](https://comphy-lab.org))

When a bubble bursts at a liquid surface, like the ones in a glass of champagne, capillary waves travel along its cavity and can focus the flow into a thin, fast Worthington jet. Although surface tension initiates this motion, the singular dynamics near jet inception are governed solely by inertia. Using direct numerical simulations and reduced-order theory, we show how the conical cavity selects a family of self-similar solutions whose exponents depend on the cone angle. The dynamics near the singularity are self-similar but not universal. For the bursting bubble geometries that I will discuss in this talk, the self-similarity exponents lie close to the classical $2/3$ value, but the local Weber number diverges as the jet radius shrinks, revealing a purely inertial force balance. Viscosity helps select the cavity geometry through capillary-wave damping. For micron-sized water bubbles, the expected regularization from viscous stresses only happen at nanometric scales where the continuum description may no longer apply [1].

We then ask how other features could regularize this singularity. For example, how this singular focusing changes when the liquid can sustain elastic stresses or resist flow below a yield stress. We show that yield stress can completely arrest cavity collapse, leaving a residual crater and suppressing jet formation [2]. Elastic stresses can also alter jet growth and breakup, allowing a jet to form without ejecting droplets [3]. In elastoviscoplastic liquids, elastic stress relaxation modifies the effect of yield stress, and jet development depends non-monotonically on the relaxation time [4]. Droplet ejection can thus be suppressed before a jet forms, by arresting cavity collapse, or after it forms, by preventing pinch-off.
